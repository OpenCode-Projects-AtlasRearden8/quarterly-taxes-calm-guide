Worksheet: Due-Date Calendar

Education only. Due dates shift when they fall on weekends and holidays, deadlines for forms and payments change from year to year, and your state runs its own calendar with its own rules. This page teaches the rhythm and gives you boxes to fill with the official dates you verify for your actual tax year, straight from the IRS website and your state tax agency website. Ten minutes of looking beats trusting a stranger's printed date, including this guide's memory of one.

Part 1: Understand the rhythm (no numbers to memorize)

The pay-as-you-go system (see the rhythm chapter) generally wants self-employed folks to make four estimated payments a year, each covering income from a rough window of the year:

PaymentThe income window it roughly coversThe usual pattern of the date
Q1roughly January through Marcharound mid-April
Q2roughly April through Mayaround mid-June
Q3roughly June through Augustaround mid-September
Q4roughly September through Decemberaround mid-January of the next year

Notice that the windows do not match calendar quarters, the date amounts and patterns are the IRS's business and not a promise, and each year's actual dates live at irs.gov and your state's tax site. Fill the next part with those actual dates.

Part 5: The anti-roll list (once per year, January-ish)

  • New year's official dates verified; reminders re-set.
  • Last year's payment confirmations moved into the year's records folder.
  • Set-aside percentage re-checked with a professional (or with real numbers) after the prior year's filing.
  • State calendar double-checked (their dates move independently).
