A Simple Quarterly Ritual

Education only. The calendar dates this ritual anchors to move slightly year to year, and the official due dates come from the IRS and your state each year — that role is filled by the due-date calendar worksheet. This chapter is about the repeatable habits that turn tax season from an annual panic into a background hum.

The ritual, in one paragraph

Four times a year, loosely anchored to the estimated-tax dates, you do three small sessions: a ledger look, a bucket sweep, and a paperwork party. Each one is short on its own, they overlap a bit in practice, and none of them require courage — just a timer and a folder. The ritual exists so that your April self meets a pile that is already organized instead of a year of mystery.

If the cashflow-simple-ledger habit is running, sessions A and B are about 15 minutes each. If your books live in a general vibe, give yourself the full hour and expect it to shrink next quarter.

Session A: The ledger look (30-45 minutes first time, less later)

Pull up your money-in, money-out, and profit since the last check-in. (This is where the cashflow-simple-ledger guide's habit pays for itself; do it there, read it here.)

  • Income since last check-in: is it steady, growing, wobbly?
  • Expenses since last check-in: anything surprising? Anything needing a receipt chase?
  • Rough profit — using the set-aside percentage, is the bucket on pace for what this quarter's rhythm suggests?
  • Any invoices sent but not yet paid?
  • Anything weird in 1099-flavored form expectations — clients who should have sent a W-9 or a form that never came?

Done when: you have written three sentences somewhere findable about how the quarter actually went. (Future-you, in April, reads them and gasps in gratitude.)

Session B: The bucket sweep (15 minutes)

Compute the set-aside on this quarter's net income with your current percentage and move the money.

StepWhat you actually do
Sum net income since last sweepLook at the ledger, not memory
Multiply by your current percentageOne multiplication, no drama
Move the moneyClick, confirm, done
Log the moveIn your tracker: date, amount, where it went
Record it as a marker for next paymentCross-reference your bucket account

If the bucket came up short: pay what you can toward the quarter, note the gap in the tracker, bump next quarter's sweep. The panic-meter worksheet has the "short bucket" version for bad days.

Done when: the money has moved and the tracker row exists.

Session C: The paperwork party (30-60 minutes around an actual due date)

When a payment date hits (or just after), paperwork gets a slot:

  • Pay (or file) what was due — through whichever channel you chose in the rhythm chapter.
  • Save the confirmation (number, date, amount) into the tracker.
  • Drop the quarter's new receipts into their monthly folders; photograph stragglers; chase any missing why notes.
  • Update the due-date calendar worksheet rows; set (or confirm) the reminders for the next date.
  • If it is winter: file incoming 1099s into the forms folder as they land; keep a short list of what you are still waiting on.

Done when: the tracker row says what was paid, when, and where the confirmation lives.

The panic-mode shortcut (10 minutes)

If the week has gone sideways and full sessions will not happen: do Session B only, at 10 minutes. Moving even part of the money beats moving nothing, and the short version keeps the thread alive. The rest happens in the next window; the ritual recovers; nobody has failed. (If the panic is bigger than the ritual, the panic-meter worksheet is the low-executive-function version of everything this guide says.)

Annual additions (twice a year, once each)

  • Year-end tidy (December, 1 hour): finish collecting receipts, close out unbilled items, update the set-aside percentage for the coming year using the year's real data, book any professional consult you will want for filing season while it is still "planning season" (see the professional chapter for why October-to-December appointments go easier than April appointments).
  • Filing season (New Year through the April filing date): the filing of the return is the W-2-flavored part of the year that needs gathering and arithmetic — the return documents the whole previous year, the estimated payments you made come off what you owe, and the session ends with an updated percentage and the four new dates on the calendar. A professional does most or all of this for many self-employed folks.

The ritual card

A pocket-sized version of all of it, to print or retype:

THE QUARTERLY RITUAL

A. Ledger look (30-45m) - income, expenses, profit, pace, weird stuff
B. Bucket sweep (15m)  - sum net, x my %, move it, log it
C. Paperwork party     - pay if due, save confirmation, file receipts,
                         update calendar, collect 1099s in winter

Panic mode: do B only. The rest waits. Still counts.

Quick recap checklist

  • Sessions A, B, C exist on my calendar (four anchor dates, verified).
  • The tracker lives where my ledger lives.
  • Year-end tidy and filing-season sessions are on the annual list.
  • Panic mode is Session B, and that is allowed.
