# Start Here (Read Me First)

> **Deep breath.** Nobody handed you a manual for this part. You are figuring out
> self-employment paperwork while running a whole business (and a whole life), and
> that is genuinely a lot. This guide exists so the paperwork part stops feeling like
> a fog and starts feeling like a short checklist you can knock out in small bites.

## What this guide is

A calm, plain-English walkthrough of the **estimated-tax rhythm** for US self-employed
people: freelancers, single-member LLC owners, partners, and side-hustle folks whose
clients do not withhold anything for them.

Specifically, it explains:

- the quarterly rhythm of estimated tax payments, and why it exists,
- how to think about **set-aside percentages** (your own number, not a magic number),
- which **receipts and records** are worth keeping, without hoarding history,
- **1099 forms decoded**, in language a friend would use,
- a **simple quarterly ritual** you can do in three small sessions,
- and **when to call a professional**, so you know you are not outsourcing your
  whole life just to ask one question.

There are two printable worksheets and a glossary in the sidebar. The worksheets are
designed to be reusable: fill them in once per quarter, photocopy or reprint as needed.

## What this guide is NOT

> **Education only.** Nothing here is tax, legal, accounting, or financial advice,
> and none of it is personalized to your situation. This guide is not affiliated
> with or endorsed by the IRS or any state tax agency. Rules, rates, thresholds, and
> dates change and differ by person. **Verify current details with the IRS and your
> state tax agency before acting**, and talk to a qualified professional (see the
> "When to call a professional" chapter) for anything that is actually yours-and-only-yours.

- **It is not tax advice.** It is the "explain it like I'm five, kindly" layer.
- **It makes no number promises.** You will not find "you will owe exactly X" claims
  anywhere in here, because honest people do not write those, and this guide would
  rather earn your trust than your click.
- **It does not file anything for you.** Tools change; this guide teaches the rhythm,
  not the button of the month.

These concepts are stable and slow-moving — the rhythm, the buckets, the records.
The actual numbers are yours to verify for your own year. That is the deal.

## Who this is for

- Self-employed people (freelancers, sole proprietors),
- single-member LLC owners whose LLC is taxed by default (like a sole proprietor),
- multi-member LLC partners and partners in partnerships (same rhythm, more forms),
- side-hustlers with a day job whose self-employment money is real income,
- and anyone recovering from "I made money this year and I have no idea if I saved
  enough."

If your LLC has an S-corp or C-corp election, or you decide to put yourself on
payroll, the `llc-first-year-handbook` covers the entity setup basics, and a
professional should tailor the payroll part. The specific rhythm explained in this
guide still applies; the plumbing is just different.

## The three eternal truths (the whole guide in one breath)

1. **Pay as you go.** Self-employed income has nothing withheld from it, so you
   pre-pay the tax along the way instead of getting one giant surprise in April.
2. **Set aside when money arrives.** When a payment lands, slice off your percentage
   into a separate place before the rest of your life eats it.
3. **Paper the story down.** Keep the receipts and records as you go, lightly, so
   that filing season becomes a tidy-and-report exercise instead of archaeology.

(That third one is the reason you will not be digging through a shoebox in April.)

## How to use this guide on a low-battery day

- Fifteen minutes: read this page, then the [due-date calendar
  worksheet](worksheets/due-date-calendar.md), and put your four dates in the calendar.
- Thirty minutes: read the rhythm chapter and pick a starting percentage.
- One full coffee: do the worksheet and print it. Tape it somewhere boring but visible.
- Everything else can wait until the first ritual.

You are not behind. You are just early in learning the rhythm, like everyone else
who ever turned a side hustle or a company into income.
