# 1099s Decoded

> **Education only.** This chapter explains what 1099 family forms are, why
> they show up, and how to react calmly when they do. It is not guidance on
> which box to mark — the forms and rules change every year, and your ledger
> plus a professional are your real safety net here.

## What a 1099 actually is

A **1099 is a payer's note about payments they made to you** — sent to you
because the payer also generally sends a copy to the IRS. It is not a bill.
It is not a prize. It is the payer saying, "hey, we paid this person this
amount this way" in the standardized way the government asks them to.

That "also goes to the IRS" part is why the forms matter even though they are
informational: the numbers usually **match** — and if your return in April
reports less income than the forms say, a mismatch is one of the easiest ways
to end up with a [CP notice](glossary.md#cp-notice) in your mailbox. Calming
thought: notices are *letters*, not ambushes, and mismatches sometimes turn
out to be resolved arithmetic. But avoidance is cheaper than cleanup, which is
why this chapter exists.

**Your ledger is the ground truth, not the forms.** Forms are somebody else's
bookkeeping copy of your income. They can arrive wrong, arrive late, arrive
not at all — the income was yours and taxable either way (see the
"I never got one" checklist below).

## The flavors you will actually meet

| Form | Rough translation | Who sends it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1099-NEC** | "non-employee compensation" — money they paid you for services as a non-employee | A client you invoiced |
| **1099-MISC** | misc other income — rents, prizes, other odd payments not NEC-shaped | A landlord-ish payer or whoever owes an odd payment |
| **1099-K** | "payment card and third-party network transactions" — money that moved through cards, apps, platforms/marketplaces | Card processors and marketplace/platform apps |
| **1099-INT** | interest your *bank* paid you | Your savings/checking accounts |
| **1099-DIV** | dividends from investments | Your brokerages |
| **1099-R** | retirement money that came out | Your retirement plan custodian |
| **W-2** | the opposite family: *your employer's* wages-and-withholding form | Your employer (if you still have a day job) |

The first three are the business ones; the last three show up because you also
have a personal financial life, and everything taxable is income, business or
personal. The W-2 is on this list for shape-recognition.

> **The 1099-K overlap thing:** when a marketplace or a payment platform is in
> the middle of a payment, you might see the same money described by a
> platform-side form and by client-side forms too. This is where ledgers save
> your sanity: you track **what you earned from whom**, and forms are just
> incoming paperwork to be reconciled. When the shapes get confusing, that is
> exactly "ask a professional" territory — not a five-minute self-audit at a
> table covered in printouts. There is a checklist for it in the worksheets.

## When they arrive (and what that means for your rhythm)

Business-flavored forms generally show up in the winter months:

- **1099-NEC** tends to be due from payers around the end of January,
- **1099-MISC** lands around the same window,
- **1099-K** is often later (frequently around the end of February or into
  March),
- and the various investment forms trail along into February and March.

**Verify the current deadline list every year** — this is a "rules change"
zone, and the exact dates shift.

The practical ritual point: **January through March, a folder fills up with
these.** The [quarterly ritual](05-quarterly-ritual.md) includes a
"paperwork party" session that naturally collects them, and the [receipts
chapter](03-receipts-what-to-keep.md) files them with the year's records.

## "I never got a 1099" — the calm checklist

- [ ] First: **income counts whether or not a form arrived.** Your ledger
      records what you actually earned. Forms are copies, not preconditions.
- [ ] Give payers a realistic window (many payers cut these around the
      January deadlines; some e-file late).
- [ ] Politely ask the payer: "could you check my 1099 went out?" Their
      records beat your speculation. Many payers can just re-late or fix.
- [ ] Check the amount if one arrived that is **wrong** — ask the payer for a
      corrected copy; keep your evidence of the real number.
- [ ] If the payer goes dark or the mismatch stays unresolved: report your
      real income based on your records, note the situation in a memo to
      yourself, and talk to a professional about the cleanest handling.
- [ ] Notice the special case: **you sent someone a W-9 and they never turned
      it into a 1099.** Still income. Same rules. Ledger rules.

## W-9: the other half of the conversation

Sometimes a client asks *you* to send them a **W-9** — that is them
collecting what they need to (a) pay you and (b) do their own reporting
correctly. It is normal, and you fill it in only when there is a real request.

Practical notes, in the "do not do extra work" spirit:

- The form itself is from the IRS; the instructions on it walk you through it.
- **If you have an active LLC and have gotten an EIN** (see the
  `llc-first-year-handbook` for that setup), use the LLC's details rather
  than your personal details whenever that is the right choice — this is a
  good thing to confirm in a professional consult, since how the numbers flow
  depends on your setup.
- W-9s contain sensitive identifiers. Send them **only where they're actually
  requested** by real clients, through real channels, and never store them in
  shared/public folders.

## Where mismatch panic goes to calm down

If a form arrives with a number that is bigger than you expected, or your
ledger disagrees with a form, or a letter arrives about it later: that is the
[mismatch checklist](worksheets/panic-meter.md) and the [notice
checklist](worksheets/panic-meter.md) — level 4 on the panic meter. Deep
breath, copy of the letter, one session, and the meter will tell you the
next step.

## Quick recap checklist

- [ ] 1099s are payer notes about payments, not bills or prizes.
- [ ] Payers generally also send IRS copies; mismatches invite notices.
- [ ] My ledger is truth; forms are copies to reconcile.
- [ ] Winter is form season; my ritual collects them without drama.
- [ ] No 1099 does not mean no income; wrong 1099 does not mean wrong filing.
- [ ] W-9s go out only to real requesters, via real channels.
