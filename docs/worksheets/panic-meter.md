# Worksheet: The Panic Meter

> **Education only.** Someone famous once said the moment you understand your
> finances is not when you understand your finances. This is a worksheet for
> the moments in between — education and self-organization, never advice. For
> anything with an IRS case number attached, or anything beyond the boring,
> see the professional chapter and bring this whole sheet.

**How to use it:** rate yourself 1 to 5 on the meter below, jump to that
level's checklist, and do *only* the next tiny step it names. You do not have
to do the whole level. The meter just turns dread into a hallway with
numbered doors.

## The meter

| Level | What it feels like | Where to go |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Vague dread, nothing wrong yet | [Level 1](#level-1-quiet-dread-nothing-on-fire) |
| 2 | A deadline is close and I am not ready | [Level 2](#level-2-deadline-close-and-i-am-behind) |
| 3 | I missed a deadline (or I think I did) | [Level 3](#level-3-i-missed-something) |
| 4 | A letter arrived from a tax agency | [Level 4](#level-4-a-letter-arrived) |
| 5 | Full spiral, cannot start anything | [Level 5](#level-5-take-five-minutes) |

---

## Level 1: quiet dread, nothing on fire

*The "it's October and I have a nameless feeling" level.*

- [ ] Name the feeling in one word if possible (late? behind? clueless?).
- [ ] Open the [due-date calendar worksheet](due-date-calendar.md). Is the
      next date closer than my gut predicted?
- [ ] Do ten minutes of [Session B energy](../05-quarterly-ritual.md): one
      sweep, one tracker row.
- [ ] Close the laptop with something *saved*. (Done is a mood of its own.)

**Done when:** one tracker row exists and the next date is on the calendar.

---

## Level 2: deadline close, and I am behind

*The "it is Tuesday the 11th and everything is due Friday" level.*

- [ ] Look up the real due date on an official source. (Half of deadline
      panic is a wrong remembered date.)
- [ ] Set-aside bucket: what is actually in it? Write the number down.
- [ ] Figure what part of the money needed is available right now.
- [ ] Move that part today. Something beats nothing when it comes to how
      penalties work — paying a partial amount generally beats paying zero
      (the rule-shape: penalties scale with how underpaid you ended up;
      verify the current rules if you want the exact math).
- [ ] Write down what remains unpaid (or unfiled) in one clear sentence, so
      future sessions start with facts instead of dread.
- [ ] If money is the problem, not willpower — this is a legitimate pro
      conversation (not an emergency; scheduling one within a couple of
      weeks is a normal, human pace).

**Done when:** whatever could be moved has been moved, and the remainder is
written down.

---

## Level 3: I missed something

*The "I opened my calendar and the due date is behind me" level.*

- [ ] **Do not panic-file.** Missed deadlines in this system mostly mean
      penalties and interest — they are costs, not catastrophes, and they are
      smaller the sooner you act. (Verify the exact cost calculation: that is
      what the IRS and a professional are for.)
- [ ] Do the Level 2 checklist as if the deadline is *now*, because the best
      time to pay late is today, not cornered-about-it-later.
- [ ] Keep confirmations of the make-up payments; they are your receipts.
- [ ] If the missed thing is something bigger (a whole unfiled year; more
      than one missed quarter), loop in a professional before more time
      passes. Unfiled-years cleanup is one of the standard jobs pros do, and
      the calm version is usually better than the cornered version.
- [ ] Close with one sentence: what was missed, what is done about it, what
      happens next. That is now a record worth its folder space.

**Done when:** the late thing is late-but-in-motion, and the paper trail exists.

---

## Level 4: a letter arrived

*The "government looking envelope" level. This is where most of the fear is.*

**First rules (the calm kind):**

- [ ] **Do not ignore it.** Notices carry real deadlines and ignoring one
      converts small problems into bigger ones. The letter itself is the
      instruction manual; read it like one.
- [ ] Check it is really from the agency (notice number, real agency
      address/phone; call the number in official records, not a number on a
      suspicious letter). The IRS generally initiates contact by mail —
      unexpected calls, texts, emails, or payment demands in gift cards are
      classic scam shapes, not IRS shapes.
- [ ] Photograph or scan the letter *front and back* (the back has
      instructions half the time).
- [ ] Find the deadline inside the letter and write it on the front in
      pen. Then only that deadline matters.

Then, by letter type:

- [ ] **Balance-due-style notice** (a number, and how they calculated it) —
      verify the math against your records and your [payments made
      list](due-date-calendar.md), then either pay it or call to question it
      (the letter itself names the channel).
- [ ] **Income-mismatch-style notice** ("we have more income than your
      return showed") — pull the questionnaire quietly: compare the
      reported numbers with your ledger, your [1099s](../04-1099s-decoded.md)
      and your filed return; if they disagree, write down where. This one is
      usually worth a professional rather than a same-day phone call.
- [ ] **Examination / audit letter** — professional time, absolutely. Do not
      respond to this one solo unless a pro tells you how.

And for all of them: log the letter, the response, and any call (who you
spoke with, when, what was said) in the same folder.

**Done when:** the letter has a copy, a deadline, and a next scheduled
action (even if that action is "professional call on Thursday").

---

## Level 5: take five minutes

*The "I cannot do executive function about this today" level. Totally
legitimate level.*

- [ ] One envelope or one folder. Everything paperwork-flavored goes in. No
      sorting, just in.
- [ ] One line in the ledger: today's date and "started." No other content
      needed.
- [ ] Five-minute timer, one small action from a lower level, only if it
      happens easily. If not, the timer ending is still a completed action.
- [ ] Body-doubling option: ask a friend to sit with you (in person or on a
      call) while each of you does your own boring task. Nothing weird about
      it; it is a standard human trick.
- [ ] If the overwhelm at paperwork is *frequent*, that is worth naming
      somewhere: a professional (taxes), yes, and/or the broader supports
      that varied humans use for executive-function struggles. Paperwork is
      a solvable system; nobody has to brute-force it forever alone.

**Done when:** there is a folder, a line, and a someday-small step written down.

---

> **A small note about this sheet:** none of it fixes anything by itself —
> that is its point. Panic does not file returns; small actions do. If the
> level you are on involves real money or real deadlines, the professional
> conversation is part of the checklist, not an extra.
